# Editorial review log

Every editorial pass is recorded here: what was found, the evidence, and what was changed.

## Review 0 — Setting up the book (2026-11-06)
- The site machinery was copied from *Introduction to LLM Engineering* (reader engine, charts, KaTeX, accounts and sync) so readers of both books get the same experience, and the authoring rules (`CLAUDE.md`) were adapted to agents: the same prose-first, motivation-first standard, the same per-chapter minimums.
- **Notation chosen up front.** Agents mix RL and language-model notation, which collide easily. Decisions: \(\pi_\theta\) is the policy (no hardware peak-FLOP symbol is needed in this book); \(\tau\) is a trajectory, so sampling temperature is \(T_s\); \(H\) is the horizon and \(t\) the agent step, so token positions use \(i\); \(\rho\) is per-step success and \(p\) per-attempt task success; \(\kappa\) is a price per token, so \(C\) is reserved for cost; \(\beta\) is the KL coefficient only.
- **Shared Firebase project.** Both books keyed progress by `ch01-mcq-01` etc., so sharing one `users/{uid}` document would have mixed them, and the LLM book's full-document write would have erased this book's data. This book now stores progress under `agenticAI`, and both books write with `mergeFields`.

## Review 1 — Chapters 1–2 self-check (2026-11-06)
- **Arithmetic re-derived before publishing.** A first draft of the Chapter 2 clarifying-question example had two hypotheses ("any Friday flight" vs "a morning flight") under which booking a morning flight satisfied both, so asking had no value; the hypotheses were changed to "cheapest" vs "before noon" so that no single booking satisfies both. The Figure 2.6 caption overstated caching savings at short horizons (3.4× at 10 steps, not 5×) and now says three to eight.
- **Factual care on history.** DQN's result is stated as the paper states it (at least 75% of a professional tester's score on 29 of 49 games), not as "superhuman on most games". SWE-bench Verified and METR time-horizon points are labelled approximate and developer-reported, with the scaffold and threshold caveats in the text.
- **Figures.** Figure numbers in Chapter 1 were reordered to match order of first mention; label collisions in Figures 1.5, 1.6 and 2.3 were fixed after looking at screenshots. Citation numbers are generated by order of first citation (`renumber` step) so inline numbers always match the reference list.

## Review 2 — Chapters 3–8 self-check (2026-11-06)
- **Word counts.** The page checker counted only visible text, omitting hidden MCQ explanations and interview answers, so chapters looked shorter than they are; it now counts all article text except diagrams and chart data. Chapters 4–8 were each extended (new subsections, drills or worked examples) to meet the 9,000-word floor and the four-diagram minimum before publishing.
- **Escaped LaTeX in scripted edits.** Inserting text through Python strings turned `\t` in `\times` and `\text{…}` into tab characters in Chapters 7 and 8; KaTeX rendered these silently as stray "imes" rather than raising an error. Fixed, and the build now fails on control characters in a chapter.
- **Notation collisions caught.** Chapter 7's revision model first used \(f,g\) for fix and break rates, colliding with the context map and parser of §2.4; renamed \(\mu,\nu\). Chapter 6's tree depth became \(\Lambda\) (\(d\) is a document). New symbols of Chapters 3–8 were added to `NOTATION.md`.
- **Facts re-checked.** PlanBench's GPT-4 figure is the ~12% average across domains reported in the paper; o1's AIME numbers are 74% single-sample and 83% consensus@64 against 12% for GPT-4o; the research-agent parallel-call example was recomputed (four times faster, not six).
